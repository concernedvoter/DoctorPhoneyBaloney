# Michigan U.S. Senate 2026: Voter Guide

As of 2026-10-22. General election November 3, 2026.

**Names.** Abdul El-Sayed's full legal name is Abdulrahman Mohamed El-Sayed (court order of December 9, 1991, Boone County, Missouri). His former name was Abdulrahman Mohamed.

**Birth, name change and Social Security.** A Boone County, Missouri court changed his name from Abdulrahman Mohamed on December 9, 1991, on a petition by a next friend, which means he was a minor (Documented). His birth date is reported as October 31, 1984. His birthplace is not independently verified: sources name Rochester Hills, Bloomfield Hills and Metro Detroit, and Michigan birth records are confidential (Reported). A blog's claim that his Social Security number was issued in Missouri in 1987 is not established, and such a number would show where an application was filed, not where he was born. None of this bears on eligibility: a U.S. birth makes a person a citizen, and no document found shows a foreign birth.

## At a glance

Republican Mike Rogers and Democrat Abdul El-Sayed are competing for Michigan's open U.S. Senate seat. Rogers runs as a national security conservative; El-Sayed runs as a progressive focused on health care and cost of living.

| | Mike Rogers (R) | Abdul El-Sayed (D) |
|---|---|---|
| Background | Army officer, FBI agent, U.S. House 2001 to 2015, House Intelligence chairman 2011 to 2015 | MD (Columbia), epidemiologist, Rhodes Scholar, former Detroit health director, 2018 governor candidate |
| Signature issue | Security, immigration enforcement, cost of living | Medicare for All, cost of living |
| Health care | Price transparency and drug costs | Single-payer Medicare for All |
| Abortion | Pro-life record; pledges not to change Michigan's amendment | Pledges to protect abortion rights |
| Guns | NRA A ratings; pro-Second Amendment | Assault weapons ban; floated amending the Constitution to replace the right to bear arms (2023) |
| Immigration | Keep ICE enforcement | Abolish ICE, return to INS; path to citizenship |
| Iran war | Backed it; now wants it ended quickly | Opposed it; diplomacy |
| Key backers | Trump, SBA Pro-Life America, gun owner groups | Sanders, UAW, AFL-CIO, Teamsters |

| Campaign money (FEC) | $23.02 million raised this cycle, $19.37 million spent, $3.90 million on hand (to Oct. 22) | $53.83 million raised, $51.10 million spent, $2.73 million on hand (to Oct. 14); outside groups had spent about $42 million for him and $95 million for Rogers by Oct. 15 |

## Record and character

Both candidates have statements that run ahead of, or conflict with, the record.

**Mike Rogers**

- Defended the CIA interrogation program in 2014 as "legal, authorized, and appropriately briefed" and called releasing the Senate report "a terrible idea." Documented.
- Moved on abortion: a long pro-life voting record, then a 2024 pledge not to change Michigan's abortion amendment. Documented.
- Points to El-Sayed's documented contacts with Brotherhood figures; his claims that El-Sayed was "bought off" or has Hamas donors had no evidence shown. Partly supported.
- Received $20,625 from private ICE detention contractors CoreCivic and GEO Group and their executives, per FEC records reviewed by The Gander. Reported.

**Abdul El-Sayed**

- Signed a November 2012 letter backing President Morsi that asked him to purge the media and police; his aides declined to answer questions about it (New York Times). Documented.
- Called Brotherhood figure Salah Soltan a friend in a 2021 post. Documented.
- Three remarks on the Second Amendment: a May 2022 video arguing it covers only a "well-regulated militia"; an April 2023 video floating its replacement with a right to health care, made private after it surfaced, which he would not disavow on Fox in August 2026; and an undated clip that circulated on Oct. 20, 2026: "When they promised the right to bear arms, they had muskets. So, yeah, everyone should have equal rights to a musket." His campaign says he "respects the Second Amendment." Reported (Fox News, The Reload, Townhall); the date of the musket clip is not established.
- At a Caro town hall, asked "Will you disavow the Muslim Brotherhood?", he answered "Sure, yeah, absolutely," then pressed the questioner on why he asked, saying the question implied dual loyalties, in a heated exchange. Documented (video, transcribed).
- A 2013 CV lists his citizenship as USA and Egypt, and in a 2016 talk he said he held dual citizenship. His campaign now says he is not a dual citizen and calls those statements a mistake. The two accounts cannot both be accurate. Documented (CV and video, via the Free Beacon).
- Presents himself as a lifelong Michigander who grew up "as Michigan as you get"; birthplace not independently verified (Bridge Michigan says Rochester Hills; the university says Bloomfield Hills), and reporting places much of his early childhood in Missouri and later Florida until at least 1995. A Boone County, Missouri court changed his name from Abdulrahman Mohamed to Abdulrahman Mohamed El-Sayed on December 9, 1991 (court docket and published notice) ([Michigan Enjoyer](https://enjoyer.com/abdul-el-sayed-parents-childhood/)). No bearing on eligibility. Name change: Documented; the rest Reported.
- Denied any family Brotherhood ties, though his half-sister wrote that their mother was active in the group in Egypt. Reported.
- Said his sister-in-law "sued and got off" the watch list; the court record does not show the suit removed her. Not supported.
- Holds an MD from Columbia but has never held a medical license. Documented.
- "As Michigan as you get" is a campaign self-description, not a birth claim. Documented: University of Michigan student and degrees (2003 to 2009), Detroit health director (2015 to 2017), Wayne County health director (2023 to 2025), and a 1991 Boone County, Missouri court name change when he was a minor. Reported: raised in Missouri, Florida and Michigan (New York Times). Not supported: that he was born outside Michigan or that the phrase was meant to mislead. No bearing on eligibility.
- Openly favors government-guaranteed health care, workers and unions over corporate and big-donor money, public health institutions, immigrants and Arab and Muslim Americans, and reproductive rights. His research also published findings that cut against several of these leanings, such as faulting Arab tradition for shorter lives. Documented.
- Eight of his statements were checked against the record. Three conflict with it or with his own other accounts: "immigrant from North Africa" (2017 speech; he is reported born in Michigan), the dual citizenship statements, and "$700 million medical debt erased" (Bridge Michigan found about $57.4 million paid off for 83,291 residents). One is Not supported as stated (the sister-in-law watch list claim). When pressed he has often answered a different question or not answered. Nothing reviewed shows intent to deceive. The same test has not been run for Rogers. Documented for the conflicts; the rest Disputed or Reported.

## The Muslim Brotherhood question

El-Sayed has documented one-step contacts with Brotherhood figures, but no evidence has been found that he or his family are members.

- **His own contacts:** the 2012 letter backing Morsi and the 2021 post calling Salah Soltan a friend. Documented.
- **Through family and CAIR:** his father-in-law led CAIR-Michigan (Reported). In 2009 a federal judge found a prima facie case of national CAIR's involvement in a conspiracy to support Hamas, then sealed the co-conspirator list; CAIR was never charged and denies any tie to Hamas. A 1994 Palestine Committee agenda lists CAIR on a line marked "Coordination." Disputed.
- **The committee itself:** the court record describes it as a Brotherhood body formed to support Hamas. Documented.
- **His response:** he denies any membership or family ties and has said "I clearly disavow" the movement. Documented.

## For Catholic voters

Rogers sits closer to Church teaching on life and family; El-Sayed sits closer on several social teaching issues. The bishops call abortion the pre-eminent priority, so that axis carries more weight.

[Graphic: Catholic compass. See deliverables/graphics/catholic-compass.md]

- **Rogers, closer:** pro-life House record, marriage, religious liberty, legitimate self-defense, enforcing immigration law (Catechism 2241). **Farther:** pledge to leave Michigan's abortion amendment alone, IVF coverage mandate, early Iran war support, 2014 defense of CIA interrogation.
- **El-Sayed, closer:** opposing the Iran war, a path to citizenship, health access, labor, creation. **Farther:** abortion rights, IVF, gender policy for minors, abolishing ICE, floating replacement of the right to bear arms, the 2012 Morsi letter, statements that conflict with the record.
- **Neither** has a stated position found on euthanasia, the death penalty or school choice.
