## Medical license and the health officer posts

**Bottom line.** Michigan law let him serve as a health officer without a medical license, as an administrative health officer working with a licensed medical director. On the public record he appears to have had less public health administrative experience than the written rule requires at both appointments, unless the state credited other experience or approved a provisional or acting appointment; those records were not found. No record reviewed shows he practiced medicine, and no complaint or charge over his use of titles has been reported. This is not legal advice.

**His credentials.** MD, Columbia, class of 2014; DPhil, Oxford. He withdrew his residency application and has never held a medical license in Michigan or New York ([Columbia](https://www.vagelos.columbia.edu/node/23958); [PolitiFact](https://politifact.com/article/2026/oct/14/abdul-el-sayed-doctor-physician-michigan-senate/)).

**The posts.** Detroit, August 2015 to February 2017: the state listed him as the Detroit Health Department's executive director and health officer ([State of Michigan](https://www.michigan.gov/formergovernors/recent/snyder/press-releases/2016/06/10/gov--rick-snyder-appoints-members-to-new-child-lead-poisoning-elimination-board)). Wayne County, 2023 to April 2025: director of Health, Human and Veterans Services and the county's health officer; Dr. Avani Sheth, the department's chief medical officer, became interim health officer when he left ([CBS Detroit](https://www.cbsnews.com/detroit/news/wayne-county-health-director-abdul-el-sayed-resigns/)).

**What the law requires**

- Each local health department must have a full-time health officer with qualifications set by the state health department ([MCL 333.2428](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=MCL-333-2428)).
- A health officer is either a medical health officer, who must be a licensed physician, or an administrative health officer ([R 325.13001 to R 325.13003](https://gsaindexed.apps.lara.state.mi.us/ORR/103_61_AdminCode.pdf)).
- An administrative health officer needs an MPH or MSPH and 3 years of full-time public health administrative experience, a related graduate degree and 5 years, or a bachelor's degree and 8 years, 5 of them administering public health programs (R 325.13003).
- When the health officer is not a physician, the department must also employ a medical director who is a licensed physician (R 325.13001 and R 325.13004).
- Provisional appointments of up to 3 years need state approval and a public health physician advisor; acting appointments run 6 months, renewable, with state approval (R 325.13007 and R 325.13008).

**How he fits.** Without a license he could not be a medical health officer. His Oxford doctorate could count as a related graduate degree, which carries the 5-year requirement. His public record shows no health department experience before Detroit and about 18 months of it before Wayne County, short even of the 3 years required with a public health master's degree. Unless the state credited other experience, such as his Columbia research program, or approved a provisional or acting appointment, he did not meet the written standard. The approval records are not online.

**Titles and the law**

- MCL 333.16263 reserves doctor of medicine and M.D. for licensees. Physician alone is not on the list, and Dr. is not restricted ([MCL 333.16263](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=MCL-333-16263)).
- MCL 333.16261 bars an unlicensed person from using any title in a way that would lead people to believe the person is licensed in Michigan ([MCL 333.16261](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=MCL-333-16261)).
- MCL 333.16294 makes practicing, or holding oneself out as practicing, a licensed health profession without a license a felony ([MCL 333.16294](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=MCL-333-16294)).
- MCL 333.16299 makes other violations of the licensing article misdemeanors, punishable on a first offense by up to 90 days in jail, a fine of up to $100, or both ([MCL 333.16299](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=MCL-333-16299)).
- Michigan's medical licensing article defines a physician as someone licensed or otherwise authorized to practice (MCL 333.17001, per PolitiFact).

**His usage.** He has called himself a physician on LinkedIn and in a March 2026 interview, and has also said he does not practice medicine; his campaign cites the American Medical Association's broader definition ([PolitiFact](https://politifact.com/article/2026/oct/14/abdul-el-sayed-doctor-physician-michigan-senate/)). A 2016 HHS roster listed him as MD, DPhil ([HHS](https://health.gov/news/blog/2016/11/meet-the-secretarys-advisory-committee-on-national-health-promotion-and-disease-prevention-objectives-for-2030)).

**Assessment.** No record reviewed shows he treated patients or prescribed, so the felony provision does not appear to reach him. The open question is whether calling himself a physician, or any use of M.D. in Michigan, would lead a reasonable person to believe he is licensed, under MCL 333.16261 and 333.16263. That is for state regulators, the attorney general or a county prosecutor to decide; none has acted or been reported to be reviewing it.
