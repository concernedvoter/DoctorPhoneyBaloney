## Stage 5: Detroit Health Department, 2015 to 2017

Mayor Mike Duggan named him the city's executive director of public health, starting August 25, 2015, at 30 ([Columbia](https://www.publichealth.columbia.edu/news/mailmans-el-sayed-takes-health-reins-detroit)); the state listed him as the department's executive director and health officer ([State of Michigan](https://www.michigan.gov/formergovernors/recent/snyder/press-releases/2016/06/10/gov--rick-snyder-appoints-members-to-new-child-lead-poisoning-elimination-board)). The city had handed nearly all of the department's work to a nonprofit by late 2012 as it headed toward bankruptcy, and Duggan began rebuilding it in 2014 ([KFF Health News via NPR](https://www.wgbh.org/news/national/2021-08-06/hard-lessons-from-detroit-a-city-that-tried-to-privatize-public-health)).

- **Record.** In 2016 the department screened tap water at all Detroit public schools for lead and copper and in August declared them lead-safe ([Michigan Public](https://www.michiganpublic.org/education/2016-08-30/city-says-drinking-water-at-all-dps-schools-now-lead-safe)); the Free Beacon later reported that lead returned in treated schools after he left, and the district shut off school drinking water in 2018 ([Free Beacon](https://freebeacon.com/democrats/abdul-el-sayed-falsely-claims-he-removed-lead-from-the-water-in-detroit-elementary-schools-when-he-was-citys-health-director/)). The city partnered with Vision To Learn on free eye exams and glasses for students ([WDET](https://wdet.org/2016/10/12/new-program-provides-free-eye-exam-and-glasses-to-dpscd-students/)). He was assigned to revamp animal control and publicly clashed with Duggan over refinery pollution ([KFF Health News via NPR](https://www.wgbh.org/news/national/2021-08-06/hard-lessons-from-detroit-a-city-that-tried-to-privatize-public-health)).
- **State and federal roles.** Gov. Rick Snyder named him to the Child Lead Poisoning Elimination Board in June 2016 ([State of Michigan](https://www.michigan.gov/formergovernors/recent/snyder/press-releases/2016/06/10/gov--rick-snyder-appoints-members-to-new-child-lead-poisoning-elimination-board)) and to the Public Health Advisory Commission that November ([Michigan Legislative Consultants](https://blog.mlcmi.com/?p=2308), summarizing the governor's announcement). HHS named him to its Healthy People 2030 advisory committee in November 2016 ([HHS](https://health.gov/news/blog/2016/11/meet-the-secretarys-advisory-committee-on-national-health-promotion-and-disease-prevention-objectives-for-2030)).
- **HIV services.** The Human Rights Campaign says he ran Detroit's Ryan White HIV program and partnered with LGBT Detroit ([HRC](https://www.hrc.org/press-releases/human-rights-campaign-pac-endorses-pro-equality-champion-abdul-el-sayed-for-u-s-senate)).
- **Citizenship claim.** In 2016 he told a GlobeMed audience in Detroit that he held dual U.S. and Egyptian citizenship, which his campaign now calls an error ([Free Beacon](https://freebeacon.com/democrats/exclusive-video-shows-abdul-el-sayed-stating-he-has-dual-citizenship-in-egypt-and-us/)).
- **Exit.** He resigned in February 2017 to run for governor ([Free Beacon](https://freebeacon.com/democrats/abdul-el-sayed-falsely-claims-he-removed-lead-from-the-water-in-detroit-elementary-schools-when-he-was-citys-health-director/); [KFF Health News via NPR](https://www.wgbh.org/news/national/2021-08-06/hard-lessons-from-detroit-a-city-that-tried-to-privatize-public-health)).

Michigan health-law note: the first federal female genital mutilation prosecution, involving Dawoodi Bohra families in Livonia, broke in April 2017, after he left office ([Justice Department](https://www.justice.gov/media/981061/dl)). This review found no public statement by him on that case.

**Health rankings during his tenure.** He led the Detroit city department, not the Wayne County Health Department, which Dr. Mouhanad Hammami headed at the time (The Nation's Health, July 2016). The County Health Rankings compare Wayne County only with other Michigan counties, and Detroit is not ranked separately. Wayne County was 83 of 83 for health outcomes and for health factors in 2016 (Detroit News, Crain's Detroit; Reported) and again in 2017 (County Health Rankings 2017 Michigan report; Documented). It was also last in 2013 and 81 of 82 on outcomes in 2011, and later releases reportedly show it last in 2020 and 2022. No county-level national ranking exists; Michigan as a state was 35th, 34th and 35th of 50 in America's Health Rankings for 2015 to 2017. The 2015 and 2018 county ranks are not yet verified.

**Outcomes versus attribution.** Detroit infant deaths per 1,000 births were 13.2 (2014), 14.4 (2015), 13.3 (2016), 14.2 (2017), 16.7 (2018) and 11.0 (2019) (Michigan Department of Health and Human Services); year-to-year changes sit within the margin of error of about 2.3, and the 2016 national rate was 5.87 (CDC). Detroit's uninsured rate fell from 19.4 percent in 2013 to 8.7 percent in 2018 (American Community Survey; University of Michigan), with the largest drop in 2014, before he arrived, driven by the Affordable Care Act and Michigan's Medicaid expansion. Elevated blood lead in children under 6 fell from 19 percent in 2009 to 9 percent in 2015 under the department's 2016 Lead Report, then rose from 7.5 to 8.8 percent in 2016 by the department's own account. The rankings use data two to four years old and his term lasted about 18 months, so they cannot show his effect. Wayne County sat at or near the bottom before, during and after his tenure. Documented outputs are the department rebuild, school water testing, Vision To Learn glasses, the demolition and lead study and animal control reform; none of the outcome changes is attributable to him. Rating: Documented, except the 2016 county rank and later years (Reported).

**Fraud and misconduct reports, 2015 to 2017.** No audit finding, investigation or charge of fraud involving him or the Detroit Health Department in this period was found in the sources reviewed. This is a limit of the search, not a clearance: not every audit was checked.

**Animal control whistleblower suit.** A former employee sued in October 2015 alleging a rodent infestation, poor animal care and too little effort to reunite animals with owners before euthanasia, and called the facility a "slaughterhouse" (Bridge Michigan). The city later settled. Judicial Watch, citing the Daily Mail, gives the amount as $63,000 and names the employee as Brittany Roberts; Bridge confirms the settlement but gives no amount. He is not named in the suit, and Bridge reports most of the problems predate animal control moving under his department in fall 2015. Rating: suit and settlement Documented; amount Reported.

**Euthanasia totals.** Republicans claim about 4,000 to 4,680 animals were euthanized during his tenure (RNC; Daily Mail). Bridge counts 2,260 in 2015, 1,107 in 2016 and 1,385 in 2017, and estimates about 1,900 during his roughly 16 months of oversight, so the Republican figure likely overstates the total by at least double. He replaced the animal control director, formed a committee of animal professionals and required 25 dogs a week be offered to rescues or shelters (Bridge). Rating: Disputed.

**Records suit and complaints.** Judicial Watch sued Detroit on October 14, 2026 (No. 26-018170) for euthanasia counts for 2010 to 2025 and health director directives for 2015 to 2017; the suit alleges no wrongdoing, only that the city did not respond to its August 14 and October 7 requests (Judicial Watch). The ACLJ filed two Michigan Freedom of Information Act requests alleging he used government posts to favor the abortion industry, but cites no contract, grant or payment (ACLJ). Rating: suit Documented; ACLJ allegation Not supported.

**Detroit Office of Inspector General.** Its second quarter 2016 report lists one initiated investigation under Health and Wellness Promotion, with no summary, status or finding; its first quarter 2017 report lists no health department cases. A Detroit FOIA request to the inspector general would show what the 2016 case was. Rating: Reported.

**Earlier context, not his tenure.** In August 2012, council members questioned about $38,000 in health department cash receipts deposited into the Institute for Population Health nonprofit's account under then director Loretta Davis, and one called for a federal investigation (CBS Detroit). No audit result or fraud finding was reported. This predates him by three years.

**Accuracy disputes, not fraud.** The Washington Free Beacon, a partisan outlet, says his claim to have "removed" lead from school water is false. In his later Wayne County role (December 2022 to April 2025), Bridge Michigan found his $700 million medical debt claim overstated, with about $95 million cleared. Rating: Disputed.

**Not reviewed.** State and federal audits of the department's grants (WIC, Ryan White, immunization), the city's response to the Judicial Watch suit, and the Snopes fact check, which would not load. Rating of this section: Documented where noted, otherwise Reported.

Rating: Documented.
