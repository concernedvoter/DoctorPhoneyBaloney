## Stage 1: Family and upbringing

He is the son of Egyptian immigrants. The University of Michigan described him in 2008 as a first-generation Egyptian American from Bloomfield Hills ([University of Michigan](https://news.umich.edu/u-m-dual-md-phd-student-named-rhodes-scholar)). Bridge Michigan gives his full name as Abdulrahman Mohamed El-Sayed, born October 31, 1984 in Rochester Hills (Bridge Michigan; Reported). The Michigan Daily named him Abdulrahman Mohamed El Sayed at his 2007 commencement, which matches the name. A birth record could not be checked, since Michigan birth records are not open to the public, and Bloomfield Hills is the hometown the university cites.

- **Father.** The Free Beacon describes him as an engineering professor at Eastern Michigan University ([Free Beacon](https://freebeacon.com/?p=2166280)). In his memoir, El-Sayed wrote that his father left Egypt in 1978 partly over political agitation against its military government ([Free Beacon](https://freebeacon.com/democrats/abdul-el-sayeds-family-has-long-been-in-egyptian-muslim-brotherhood-senate-candidates-sister-said-in-unearthed-social-media-posts/)).
- **Mother.** She and his father divorced when he was about three ([Free Beacon](https://freebeacon.com/democrats/exclusive-video-shows-abdul-el-sayed-stating-he-has-dual-citizenship-in-egypt-and-us/)). The New York Times adds that he was raised in Missouri, Florida and Michigan by his father and a stepmother, and that she is now a psychiatric nurse practitioner in Missouri (New York Times, Oct 21, 2026). Her later daughter, his half-sister Eman Abdelhadi, is a University of Chicago professor whose posts are covered in the Brotherhood section.
- **Egypt.** He has spoken of childhood visits to his father's family in Alexandria ([Free Beacon](https://freebeacon.com/?p=2166901)).
- **Hometown****.** Bloomfield Hills, per the University of Michigan's 2008 release ([University of Michigan](https://news.umich.edu/u-m-dual-md-phd-student-named-rhodes-scholar)).

**Name and court records (added October 23, 2026).** Missouri court and newspaper records now document his name history. They do not document his birthplace.

- **Name until December 1991.** Boone County case 1391CC044966, *In re Abdulrahman Mohamed*, was filed November 14, 1991 by Abdulrahman Mohamed "by Next Friend Mohamed El-Sayed" and decided without contest on December 9, 1991: the name was changed to Abdulrahman Mohamed El-Sayed ([Missouri CaseNet docket](https://www.courts.mo.gov/casenet/), which states it is not an official court record; the court's published notice ran December 20 and 27, 1991 and January 3, 1992, newspaper not yet identified). A next friend acts for a minor, so he was a minor then. The filings do not state the relationship or his age. Documented as to the name; the surname on his birth record is not in these filings.
- **Parents' records, context only.** A 1988 Boone County domestic-relations docket (1388DR026716) records a stipulated modification of a dissolution decree as to child custody and support, and a judgment of October 11, 1988 ending one party's support obligation. The child is not named and the docket does not show who had custody. It fits the report that his parents divorced when he was young. Reported.
- **Naturalization records, context only.** Federal court records show two adults with the names of his father and mother were admitted to citizenship on March 22, 1990 in Kansas City and lived in Columbia, Missouri. His mother's petition was filed under the five-year route, so she was a permanent resident for at least five years before 1990. Her entry date is not public. The name match is an inference; no document states the relationship. Reported.
- **What this does not show.** None of it states where he was born or bears on his citizenship. A person born in the United States is a citizen at birth, and nothing here shows a foreign birth.
- **How to resolve the rest.** The court file for 1391CC044966 (petition and proof of publication) is released by the Boone County Circuit Clerk on request. Birth records are sealed and are not sought.
