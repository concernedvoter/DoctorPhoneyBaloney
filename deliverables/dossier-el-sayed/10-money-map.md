## Money map

CAIR-linked money reached his race through three channels: CAIR staff giving to his campaign, the CAIR-endorsed Unity and Justice Fund, and his father-in-law. FEC filings, read line by line on October 18, 2026, show the father-in-law's $300,000 and the fund's $175,000 made up about 11 percent of the $4.4 million Fighting for Michigan raised through August 31. His campaign raised $30.7 million in one quarter, more than a third from donors giving under $200.

**FEC update, October 26, 2026.** Through the pre-general reports (coverage to October 14), his campaign committee, Abdul for U.S. Senate (C00902668), shows $53.83 million in cycle receipts, $51.10 million spent and $2.73 million cash on hand, with no debts ([FEC](https://www.fec.gov/data/committee/C00902668/)). Rogers's two authorized committees together show $23.02 million raised, $19.37 million spent and $3.90 million on hand ([FEC](https://www.fec.gov/data/candidate/S4MI00595/)). The cutoffs differ (his October 14, Rogers's FEC date October 22), so compare with care. In the first two weeks of October he raised $8.66 million and spent $9.71 million ([FEC pre-general report](https://docquery.fec.gov/cgi-bin/forms/C00902668/2039805/)); Rogers raised nearly $2.5 million ([Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/10/23/senate-democrats-fundraising-october-midterm-elections/)). Method and open reconciliations are in records/fec-method.md.

Super PACs spend independently and may not coordinate with the campaign. The campaign refuses corporate PAC money but accepts union and advocacy PACs ([Bridge Michigan](https://bridgemi.com/uncategorized/abdul-el-sayed-sets-michigan-fundraising-record-see-whos-contributing/)).
