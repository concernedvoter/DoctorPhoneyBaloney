## Muslim Brotherhood: claims and evidence

No reporting reviewed here documents his own membership in, or pledge to, the Brotherhood. The live questions are his past writing on Egypt, a reported 2012 letter, his friendship with Mohamed Soltan, and his mother's side of the family.

**Senate hearing, August 2026.** The day after the August 4 primary, Sen. Ted Cruz chaired a Senate Judiciary subcommittee hearing titled "Hidden in Plain Sight: Confronting the Muslim Brotherhood Network in America." No Democrats attended ([hearing video](https://youtu.be/DAzJC6ZkVGk); [JNS](https://www.jns.org/news/u-s-news/cruz-accuses-el-sayed-of-links-to-muslim-brotherhood)). Cruz called El-Sayed the son-in-law of a leading member of several Brotherhood groups, and said without offering evidence that his campaign was embracing terrorist organizations (6:18 to 6:53). Witness Kyle Shideler of the Center for Security Policy cited his father-in-law's CAIR-Michigan and ISNA roles and his ties to Emgage (43:21). Witness Ariel Klepak of the National Jewish Advocacy Center cited his response to the March 2026 Temple Israel attack, which he later apologized for (1:00:07). Laura Burns, the former FBI agent who led the Holy Land Foundation case, did not discuss him. The witnesses were sworn, but all were invited by the majority and none was cross-examined. Rating: Documented as statements; the hearing added no evidence of a personal tie.

**Gateway Pundit, "Man Convicted in Egypt for Funding the Muslim Brotherhood Now Pumping Cash Into the Abdul El-Sayed Campaign" (Mike LaChance, Oct. 28, 2026; checked Oct. 29, 2026).** The piece is derivative. It reposts The Midwesterner's August 2026 article, which cites NBC News for the 2015 sentence, and embeds an X post about his citizenship. It cites no filings and gives no dollar amounts or dates. Headline: partly true. The man is Mohamed Soltan, not his father, and the money is real. Three framing points conflict with the record. Relatives are context only.

Weakest link: "funding the Muslim Brotherhood." The charge as reported by wire services was financing a sit-in, and a rights group calls the case political. Overall rating: Disputed. Apply the same donor test to Rogers' committees (see section 19). Open: the Egyptian judgment itself and the 2017 retrial record.

**Assessment.** The record supports a reported family connection on his mother's side and associations with people and groups that critics tie to the Brotherhood. It does not show him joining, funding or pledging to the Brotherhood.

**Public perception and bias metrics.** An MSU survey found 37% of likely 2026 general-election voters said they are less likely to support a Muslim candidate; political scientist Matt Grossmann noted such attacks may gain traction when they appear to work (Bridge Michigan, Sept. 3, 2026). CAIR-MI reported three death threats against the organization in three consecutive days following an anti-Islam protest in Dearborn over the same period. On the Brotherhood attacks specifically, he has responded with pointed rhetoric rather than a detailed rebuttal, including the line that "if there's any jihad, it's going to be the one against medical debt." Rating: survey figure and threat reports Documented (Bridge Michigan); no detailed point-by-point rebuttal from the campaign found.
