## Stage 3: Oxford and Columbia, 2009 to 2014

He earned a DPhil from Oxford as a Rhodes Scholar and an MD from Columbia, class of 2014, but withdrew his residency application and has never held a medical license in Michigan or New York ([Columbia](https://www.vagelos.columbia.edu/node/23958); [Politi](https://politifact.com/article/2026/oct/14/abdul-el-sayed-doctor-physician-michigan-senate/)[Fact](https://politifact.com/article/2026/oct/14/abdul-el-sayed-doctor-physician-michigan-senate/)).

- **Oxford.** Columbia describes the degree as a DPhil in population health, earned as a Rhodes Scholar ([Columbia](https://www.publichealth.columbia.edu/news/mailmans-el-sayed-takes-health-reins-detroit)).
- **Columbia.** MD from the College of Physicians and Surgeons, where he was a Paul and Daisy Soros Fellow ([Columbia](https://www.publichealth.columbia.edu/news/mailmans-el-sayed-takes-health-reins-detroit); [Soros Fellowships](https://pdsoros.org/fellows/abdul-el-sayed/)).
- **Soros link.** The Paul and Daisy Soros Fellowships for New Americans fund graduate study by immigrants and the children of immigrants ([Soros Fellowships](https://pdsoros.org/)). Their son Jeffrey Soros, a nephew of George Soros, hosted a 2026 fundraiser for him and said he had interviewed him as a finalist ([TNND via WJLA](https://wjla.com/news/nation-world/george-soros-nephew-holds-fundraiser-for-abdul-el-sayed-says-family-paid-his-tuition-michigan-senate-race-campaign-mike-rogers-hollywood)).
- **Writing on Egypt.** A 2011 HuffPost column urged U.S. support for Egypt's protesters and argued that even a Brotherhood-led government would not resemble Iran ([Bridge Michigan](https://www.bridgemi.com/michigan-government/fact-check-no-evidence-abdul-el-sayed-pledged-support-to-muslim-brotherhood/)). A 2012 group letter backing President Morsi is attributed to him; see the Brotherhood section.
- **CNN essay, September 2012.** He argued Muslim Americans were among the victims of the post-9/11 climate and cited a California study of worse birth outcomes. His own 2008 Michigan study had found no change after 9/11, a gap the Free Beacon highlighted ([Free Beacon](https://freebeacon.com/democrats/abdul-el-sayed-to-join-panel-with-9-11-conspiracy-theorists-at-islamic-convention-days-before-9-11-anniversary/)).
- **The Root essay, March 2012.** Under the byline Abdulrahman El-Sayed, a piece first written for PolicyMic and republished by The Root argued that the killings of Trayvon Martin and Shaima Alawadi showed how symbols such as hoodies and hijabs feed fear of "others", and that blame reaches beyond the killers to institutions that reinforce stereotypes. It does not mention Hamas, the Brotherhood or Egypt, so it adds nothing on those questions. It was written weeks after Alawadi's death, when it was widely reported as a possible hate crime; her husband was later arrested and convicted of the murder (The Root, March 28, 2012; SPLC; NBC San Diego). Rating: Documented as his writing; the later case outcome is context, not a charge against him.
- **Reuters quote, March 31, 2012.** A Reuters story on the Alawadi killing quoted "Abdulrahman El-Sayed, a fellow at think tank Demos and an epidemiologist at Columbia University," saying the Alawadi and Trayvon Martin cases, despite their differences, highlight broader discrimination against black and Muslim communities. The story, by Mary Slosson, notes police were investigating a possible hate crime but would not label it one, and nothing in it concerns Hamas, the Brotherhood or Egypt (Reuters). It shows he was a public commentator on these issues by 2012. Rating: Documented.
- **Regents' resolution, December 18, 2008.** The University of Michigan Board of Regents honored him as a Rhodes Scholar in a resolution read by Regent Katherine White, recorded in the minutes approved in January 2009. It names him Abdulrahman M. ElSayed, calls him a Bloomfield Hills native, and cites his 2007 biology and political science degree, Angell Scholar and Phi Beta Kappa recognition, lacrosse, his 2007 commencement address that impressed President Clinton, a medical mission to Peru, a neurosurgery interest group he founded, and leadership of the Muslim Students Association and election as president of the Muslim Medical Students Association. He thanked his mentors and said he would return from Oxford to finish his M.D. and Ph.D. Chancellor Person of U-M Flint also congratulated him in her report and noted his mother is a colleague of hers (Board of Regents minutes). The Bloomfield Hills hometown differs from the Rochester Hills birthplace Bridge Michigan gives. Rating: Documented.
- **Michigan Daily archive search, 2003 to 2010.** A search of the Michigan Daily's digitized archive for El-Sayed, ElSayed and El Sayed returned 13 pages. The relevant items: an October 19, 2006 feature on young married Muslim couples that profiled him and Sarah Jukaku (the article says he gave up Michigan lacrosse and would start medical school the next year); a January 31, 2007 report naming "Muslim Student Association Vice President Abdul-Rahman El Sayed" as speaking at a counter-event after a walkout of a Young Americans for Freedom event; his May 1, 2007 commencement address, where the Daily names him Abdulrahman Mohamed El Sayed, matching the full name Bridge Michigan gives; and the November 24, 2008 Rhodes story, with a December 8, 2008 reader letter correcting the Daily's use of the word "girlfriend" for Sarah Jukaku before their marriage. Pages also matched with other items about Gaza, but only because they shared a page; a search of his name together with Hamas, Brotherhood, Hezbollah or Palestinian in this window found no article connecting him to those groups. Some matches concern other people with the same surname, including a Wafd party figure in an Egyptian election story and a student who founded a Peru aid group, and are not attributed to him (Michigan Daily Digital Archives, Bentley Historical Library). Rating: Documented.
- **Citizenship.** A CV bearing the date October 2013 (published by the Free Beacon) lists his citizenship as USA and Egypt ([Free Beacon, Jan 9, 2026](https://freebeacon.com/democrats/michigan-senate-candidate-abdul-el-sayed-identified-himself-as-an-egyptian-citizen-when-he-became-a-columbia-professor-now-his-campaign-says-he-made-a-mistake/)), and in a 2016 talk in Detroit he said he carried dual citizenship ([Free Beacon](https://freebeacon.com/democrats/exclusive-video-shows-abdul-el-sayed-stating-he-has-dual-citizenship-in-egypt-and-us/)[, Oct 5, 2026](https://freebeacon.com/democrats/exclusive-video-shows-abdul-el-sayed-stating-he-has-dual-citizenship-in-egypt-and-us/)). His campaign says he is not a dual citizen by any verifiable metric: as a child he was told his grandfather had pursued Egyptian citizenship for him, and the family found no documents. It has not said when or how he established that. No Egyptian government record has been reported either way. Egypt's nationality law makes a child born to an Egyptian father Egyptian by birth, and an Egyptian who takes another nationality without government permission remains Egyptian until the cabinet strips it, so a lack of documents does not by itself settle how Egypt regards him ([Law No. 26 of 1975, via Refworld](https://www.refworld.org/legal/legislation/natlegbod/1975/en/17434); [EUAA, 2022](https://www.ecoi.net/en/file/local/2068807/2022_02_Q16_EUAA_COI_Query_Response_Egypt_Citizenship_Law.pdf)). Decrees granting permission for a foreign nationality, or stripping Egyptian nationality, must be published in Egypt's Official Journal. Dual nationality would not bar him from the Senate, which requires only age 30, nine years of U.S. citizenship and residence in the state ([U.S. Constitution, art. I, sec. 3](https://constitution.congress.gov/browse/article-1/section-3/)).
- **Egyptian law, 2000 to 2012.** The Egyptian embassy's consular services cover passports, ID cards, nationality and dual-citizenship approvals for the person concerned, with no public search of anyone's status ([Embassy of Egypt](https://egyptembassy.net/consular-services/)). Egypt's 1975 nationality law made anyone born to an Egyptian father Egyptian by birth ([Law No. 26 of 1975, via Refworld](https://www.refworld.org/legal/legislation/natlegbod/1975/en/17434)). In this period the State Department advised that Egypt considered all children of Egyptian fathers Egyptian citizens even without an Egyptian birth certificate or passport, as quoted in a 2013 Defense Department ruling ([DOHA, ADP Case No. 11-10625](https://doha.ogc.osd.mil/Industrial-Security-Program/Industrial-Security-Clearance-Decisions/ISCR-Hearing-Decisions/Archived-ISCR-Hearing-Decisions/2016-and-Prior-ISCR-Hearing-Decisions-9/FileId/135938)). On that rule he was an Egyptian national from birth if his father was Egyptian when he was born. Article 10's permission rule covers Egyptians who acquire another nationality later, and he was born with U.S. citizenship. If his father had given up Egyptian nationality before he was born, a 2004 amendment let children of Egyptian mothers born earlier apply for recognition through the mother ([EUDO Citizenship](https://globalcit.eu/?p=8111)). Under either route he was eligible in this period; whether he registered or was issued Egyptian documents is not known.

Rating: Documented, except the 2012 letter (Reported).
