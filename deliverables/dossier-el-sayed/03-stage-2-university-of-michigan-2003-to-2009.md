## Stage 2: University of Michigan, 2003 to 2009

At Michigan he gave the 2007 student commencement address, and served as Muslim Students' Association vice president in his senior year.

- **Degree.** Bachelor's in biology and political science, 2007. That spring he gave a student commencement address, which Bill Clinton, the main speaker, praised ([University of Michigan](https://news.umich.edu/u-m-dual-md-phd-student-named-rhodes-scholar)).
- **Muslim Students' Association.** Vice president in 2006 to 2007 ([College Fix, via Legal Insurrection](https://legalinsurrection.com/2026/08/abdul-el-sayed-protested-conservative-event-condemning-islamic-terrorism-as-an-undergrad/)); the university's 2008 release called him an active member ([University of Michigan](https://news.umich.edu/u-m-dual-md-phd-student-named-rhodes-scholar)). In January 2007 he joined a walkout of a Young Americans for Freedom event featuring three self-described former militants, and spoke at the counter-event.
- **Marriage.** His father-in-law is Dr. Tayeb Jukaku, who appears in Stage 8 and the money map ([Free Beacon](https://freebeacon.com/democrats/abdul-el-sayeds-father-in-law-a-top-donor-to-an-el-sayed-super-pac-is-among-top-leaders-of-muslim-brotherhood-organization-linked-to-terror-funding/)).
- **Medical school.** In 2008 he was pursuing an MD at the U-M Medical School and a PhD in epidemiology through the dual-degree Medical Scientist Training Program ([University of Michigan](https://news.umich.edu/u-m-dual-md-phd-student-named-rhodes-scholar)).
- **Rhodes.** Named a Rhodes Scholar in November 2008, one of 32 nationwide, days after being offered a Marshall Scholarship ([University of Michigan](https://news.umich.edu/u-m-dual-md-phd-student-named-rhodes-scholar)).
- **Michigan Record profile, November 24, 2008.** The university's article on his Rhodes award says he planned a career in neurosurgery, played on the men's lacrosse team, was active in the Muslim Students' Association, co-founded a student group that raised over $4,500 for a local clinic, and led a medical mission to Peru. His research then covered the social determinants of health, Arab-American health and neural tube defects in Guatemala (University of Michigan Record). Rating: Documented.
- **First research.** With Sandro Galea at Michigan's School of Public Health, he published on Arab-American birth outcomes after 9/11 (2008) and on Arab-American health (2009). Details in Stage 4.
- **Faith in practice.** In 2009 he told the New York Times he had chosen a Sharia-compliant mortgage for an Ann Arbor condo ([Washington Times](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2026/aug/25/el-sayeds-sharia-canon-law-comparison-draws-swift-conservative/)). See the Sharia section.

Rating: Documented.

**What the two pieces show about his views then.** He wrote as a community organizer focused on Muslim unity and outreach, as a critic of the Iraq war, and as someone who condemned violence while explaining it by reference to the war. His 2006 sentence on the kidnappers' "ends" can be read two ways: as saying he does not dispute that releasing detained Iraqi women is a legitimate demand, or, as critics read it, as sympathy with the captors' goals. The column condemns the act and does not endorse it. He was about 21.

**What they do not show.** Neither piece mentions the Muslim Brotherhood, endorses a designated group, calls for an Islamic state, or says anything about Israel. The IPT itself concedes that "Regional Unity" does not endorse the Brotherhood or call for an Islamic state.

**The IPT's Part 3, "El-Sayed in the Brotherhood's Orbit: The Ann Arbor Years" (Oct. 28, 2026, Steven Emerson).** It says it recovered MSA chapter newsletters and board minutes from 2004 to 2007. We have not seen the minutes. Its claims about him, as the article states them:

- Outreach co-chair for 2004 to 2005 who led high school recruitment and attended at least eight meetings: Reported.
- Attended the first board meeting of his outreach year, which planned a "call to action" week against the visa revocation of scholar Tariq Ramadan: Reported.
- The chapter's board discussed a KindHearts charity fundraising dinner in December 2004 and January 2005, and he "appears to have attended" a December meeting and was present in January; the minutes do not show whether the dinner took place: Reported, with the article's own uncertainty. The article says Treasury froze KindHearts' assets in 2006 without finalizing a designation and that a 2012 settlement ended the matter; those points were not checked here.
- Vice president for internal affairs in 2006 to 2007, recorded in section 03 above from other sources: Reported.
- The chapter and MSA National are "in the Brotherhood's orbit," a characterization built on the national MSA's role in founding ISNA, speaker invitations and government lists: opinion; Disputed. ISNA's listing on the 2007 Holy Land Foundation co-conspirator filing is Documented elsewhere in this dossier, and it is a list in a filing, not a charge.
- The article does not claim he joined the Brotherhood, supported Hamas or committed a crime, and it says the minutes do not show what he said at outreach sessions.

**Weakest link.** Attendance at student meetings, to chapter, to national MSA, to Brotherhood influence. The documents show participation in a mainstream campus religious group. They do not show membership in or a pledge to the Brotherhood, so the finding in section 12 is unchanged. Open: obtain the original newsletters and minutes, read the other five parts of the IPT series, and ask the campaign about the 2006 column.

**Chapter structure, not him.** The IPT quotes the chapter's 2006 constitution as making it a chapter of the national MSA and a Great Lakes council, with an "Emir" as president, a "Council of Consultation," a requirement that members attest to the Shahada, and oath wording from a hadith. It quotes MSA National's 2006 site as saying it "gave birth to" ISNA, a 2007 FBI assessment calling the MSA "the first formal U.S. MB organization," and a 1991 Brotherhood memo listing the MSA among "our organizations and the organizations of our friends." None of these was seen here, and none says what he believed or did. Titles and Islamic terms in a religious student group's constitution are not evidence of Brotherhood membership.

**How to read it.** The pattern the IPT draws is a mainstream, outreach-oriented campus religious group whose national parent and speakers have Brotherhood-linked critics. The items about him show participation in recruitment, outreach, protests and an Iraq-war and Palestine advocacy agenda, alongside a public condemnation of the Carroll kidnapping and a vigil for her. They do not show a pledge, membership or support for violence, and the IPT does not claim they do. Open: obtain the minutes and newsletters, which would settle what is attributed to him.
