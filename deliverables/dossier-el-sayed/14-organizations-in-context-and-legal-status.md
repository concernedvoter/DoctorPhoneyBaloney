**Life for Relief and Development (LRD), Southfield, Michigan (added October 29, 2026, expanded after a public-records check).** The Free Beacon (Jon Levine, Oct. 28, 2026) reports that the candidate's father sits on the board of this charity. His father is a relative, so this is context only; it is not an allegation about the candidate. LRD and the father did not respond to the Free Beacon, and the campaign gave no on-the-record response.

- **Father on the board: Documented that he serves; relationship Reported.** LRD's Form 990s list Dr. Mohammed El-Sayed as an unpaid director from fiscal 2020 through 2024, and LRD's site lists him as an Eastern Michigan University engineering professor and visiting imam. Neither says he is the candidate's father; that match rests on the name and profession already described in Stage 1. His service began about 17 years after the sanctioned conduct. His son's father-in-law, Dr. Tayeb Jukaku, appears as a director on the fiscal 2019 form only.
- **Treasury settlement: Documented.** On March 25, 2015, LRD agreed to pay $780,000 (base penalty $1.3 million) to the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control for apparent violations of the Iraqi Sanctions Regulations between September 9, 2002 and March 19, 2003. OFAC found a conspiracy to transfer funds to Iraq through Amman, Jordan, counted three transfers totaling $236,000, called it an egregious case, said LRD's president knew of and directed the transfers, and found no voluntary self-disclosure. It was part of a cooperation agreement with the Justice Department, whose terms are not public. No Treasury or State terrorism designation of LRD was found. The settlement names no board members.
- **How the article ties LRD to IARA: a co-mention, not a connection.** One sentence says 2006 searches of LRD sought records "related to" the Islamic African Relief Agency. A separate sentence says that agency was a known alias for the group where the candidate's mother worked, citing Treasury's 2004 designation, which does not mention LRD. The search overlap is Reported: the Detroit News, as copied on a partisan site, said the warrants sought records related to several named organizations, and the warrant itself was not obtained. Any organizational connection (shared officers, directors, staff, address, funds or transfers) is Not supported: none was found in Treasury's 2004 release, OFAC's 2015 notice, Justice Department releases on IARA, or LRD's tax forms. LRD's spokesman said at the time that the agencies named were based in the United Arab Emirates, and LRD officials said federal officials told them the inquiry was not related to terrorism; both are LRD's account and are unverified.
- **Other leaders and the Brotherhood directory: Reported.** The Free Beacon and other accounts say CEO Hany Saqr and board member M. Yahia Abdul-Rahim appear in a 1992 directory of U.S. Brotherhood leaders introduced in the Holy Land Foundation trial. We have not read that exhibit. A listing in a seized directory is not a court finding of membership, and neither man was charged in that case. A Sherif Gindy appears as a director on LRD's forms through fiscal 2019 and not afterward, so the article's present-tense "board member" is Disputed; the statements it attributes to him cite no source: Not supported.
- **Raids, charges and banks.** A 2006 FBI and IRS search of LRD's offices and the home of its then-CEO is Reported, as is a 2004 U.S. military raid of its Baghdad office. The claim that a grand jury charged the CEO in 2007 is Not supported (no indictment found). The claim that the case was "dropped in 2014" is Disputed (OFAC announced the settlement in March 2015). The bank-closure account is Disputed as to timing: Comerica moved to close accounts in 2006, and Bank of America gave notice in 2012; a jury found for the bank in 2016. A 2007 New York Post report, citing unnamed law-enforcement sources, said LRD gave millions to Hamas's Jordanian operation; no charge followed: Reported, single outlet.
- **Al-Hanooti: Reported.** CNN reported that Muthanna Al-Hanooti was LRD's public relations director on and off from 1999 to 2006 and that, per his indictment, Iraqi intelligence paid $34,000 through an intermediary to LRD for a 2002 congressional delegation's travel. The Justice Department said no members of Congress were accused. He pleaded guilty to one count of contracting to buy Iraqi oil and was sentenced on March 18, 2011 to a year and a day. The judgment itself does not mention LRD.
- **Mother: no link to LRD found.** Her IARA-USA job from about 1999 to 2004 is documented elsewhere in this dossier; the New York Times and Snopes report she was never accused. No document ties her to LRD: Not supported. Separately, a Columbia, Missouri LRD fundraiser, Shakir Hamoodi, pleaded guilty in 2009 to sending about $200,000 to Iraq in violation of sanctions and was sentenced in 2012; no document ties him to IARA.
- **Donations and the 2012 letter: not verified.** The article says Saqr, Abdul-Rahim and Gindy each gave $1,000 to the Senate campaign and that Saqr signed the 2012 letter to President Morsi. Neither has been checked against FEC records or the letter's signature list.

Weakest link: any bearing of LRD's past or its leaders' alleged ties on the father, and through him the candidate. The documents show a civil sanctions settlement by the charity and a father who serves as an unpaid director now. They do not show wrongdoing by the father or the candidate, and they show no organizational connection between LRD and IARA. Open: the 2006 search-warrant dockets and returns (E.D. Mich.), LRD's full Form 990 board and grant schedules for earlier years, Michigan corporate records, the 1992 directory exhibit, the FEC records for the gifts, and LRD's and the father's responses.
