**Life for Relief and Development (LRD), Southfield, Michigan (added October 29, 2026).** The Free Beacon (Jon Levine, Oct. 28, 2026) reports that the candidate's father sits on the board of this charity. His father is a relative, so this is context only; it is not an allegation about the candidate, and LRD and the father did not respond to the Free Beacon and the campaign gave no on-the-record response.

- **Father on the board: Reported.** LRD's website lists "Dr. Mohammed Elsayed," professor of engineering at Eastern Michigan University and a visiting imam. The page does not say he is the candidate's father; the match rests on the name and profession already described in Stage 1. The Free Beacon says federal tax forms show his role since at least 2020. His son's father-in-law, Dr. Tayeb Jukaku, is reported to have served on the board earlier.
- **Treasury settlement: Documented.** On March 25, 2015, LRD agreed to pay $780,000 (base penalty $1.3 million) to the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control for apparent violations of the Iraqi Sanctions Regulations between September 9, 2002 and March 19, 2003. OFAC found a conspiracy to transfer funds to Iraq through Amman, Jordan, counted three transfers totaling $236,000, called it an egregious case, and found no voluntary self-disclosure. It credited LRD with remedial steps, a new compliance program and a cooperation agreement with the Justice Department. The settlement names no board members, and nothing shows the father served on the board in 2002 to 2003.
- **Brotherhood links of other board members: Reported.** The Free Beacon says its CEO, Hany Saqr, and board member M. Yahia Abdul-Rahim appear in a 1992 directory of U.S. Brotherhood leaders that prosecutors introduced in the Holy Land Foundation trial. We have not read that exhibit. A listing in a seized directory is not a court finding of membership, and the article's board list includes a Sherif Gindy who is not on LRD's board page. The article's statements attributed to Gindy cite no source: Not supported.
- **Raids and charges: Reported.** The article cites newspapers for a 2004 U.S. military raid of LRD's Baghdad offices, a 2006 FBI raid, a 2007 indictment of its former CEO, and a 2014 end to the case under an agreement. None of those documents was read here. A separate Eastern District of Michigan judgment (No. 08-20083, March 2011) records that Muthanna Al-Hanooti pleaded guilty to one count of contracting to buy Iraqi oil under the sanctions law and received 12 months and a day. That judgment does not mention LRD, so the article's description of him as LRD's public relations coordinator is Reported only.
- **Mother's IARA job: no link to LRD found.** The article connects LRD to the Islamic African Relief Agency, where his mother worked. Treasury's October 13, 2004 designation of the Islamic African Relief Agency (also known as Islamic American Relief Agency) does not mention LRD. The link is not shown; her employment is covered in the gaps section.
- **Donations and the 2012 letter: not verified.** The article says Saqr, Abdul-Rahim and Gindy each gave $1,000 to the Senate campaign and that Saqr signed the 2012 letter to President Morsi. We have not checked either against FEC records or the letter's signature list.

Weakest link: whether LRD's past or any board member's alleged ties reflect on the father, and through him the candidate. The documents show a civil sanctions settlement by the charity and a father who serves on its board now. They do not show wrongdoing by the father or the candidate. Open: read the 1992 directory entry, the LRD Form 990 board list by year, the donors in FEC itemized records, and LRD's and the father's responses.
