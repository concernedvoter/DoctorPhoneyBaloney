**Court documents on the co-conspirator list and a CAIR co-founder (read in full October 22).** Three filings now sit in the repo's public-records folder. (1) United States v. Holy Land Foundation (North American Islamic Trust, movant), No. 09-10875, Fifth Circuit, October 20, 2010: the government's 2007 trial brief attached a list of 246 unindicted co-conspirators and joint venturers, filed publicly by what the government called an oversight, with the Trust listed under a heading for people or groups who "are and/or were members of the U.S. Muslim Brotherhood"; the brief called Hamas the Palestinian branch of the Brotherhood. The Fifth Circuit affirmed that the listing was a procedural error because the list should have been sealed, ordered the district court's opinion unsealed, and let stand the refusal to remove the Trust's name. It also held that the Trust's inclusion was "simply an untested allegation of the Government," that no co-conspirator ruling about the Trust was ever made, and that the district judge's statement of "ample evidence" linking the Trust to the Holy Land Foundation, the Islamic Association for Palestine and Hamas went outside the bounds of what was needed and was not grounded in a legal standard with substance and boundaries. It said the findings do not amount to a ruling that the Trust joined a criminal conspiracy, and the government itself disclaimed calling the Trust a criminal co-conspirator. A footnote says CAIR moved to file a brief asking that all names be stricken, that the motion was addressed in the same sealed order, and that CAIR was not a party to the appeal. (2) United States v. El-Mezain, No. 09-10560, Fifth Circuit, December 7, 2011 (170 pages, read in full): it does not name CAIR, but it says Omar Ahmad was a member of the Palestine Committee and associated with the Islamic Association for Palestine, and that 1999 wiretaps caught him directing Holy Land Foundation business, which the court took to suggest the committee controlled the foundation even after Hamas was designated in 1995. (3) Judicial Watch v. Department of Justice, 898 F. Supp. 2d 93 (D.D.C. Oct. 12, 2012), a records case that describes Omar Ahmad as a co-founder of CAIR; it notes the government has never formally pursued his prosecution, and the court accepted the Justice Department's refusal to confirm or deny any records of a decision not to prosecute him. Taken together: Documented that a CAIR co-founder is described by the Fifth Circuit as a Palestine Committee member, which the same opinion says the Brotherhood directed to support Hamas; Documented that CAIR appears in the co-conspirator controversy; Not supported that any court convicted CAIR or found it to be a criminal co-conspirator (Judge Solis found only a prima facie case of its involvement, described in the next paragraph); the ruling's "ample evidence" language, which JTA reports covered CAIR, the Islamic Society of North America and the Trust together, was criticized on appeal as going beyond the legal question, and the order itself (Document 1356, July 1, 2009) has now been read and is summarized in the next paragraph; JTA adds that the evidence the judge cited largely predates the foundation's 2001 designation, that some of it is shared officers and office space in the late 1980s, and that a 1991 Brotherhood strategy paper lists the Society and the Trust, but not CAIR, among its "friends". Ahmad was never charged, and the Palestine Committee findings are about the 1980s and 1990s. Nothing in these filings mentions Abdul El-Sayed, his father-in-law or CAIR-Michigan; CAIR-Michigan is a separate chapter from the national group Ahmad helped found. Rating: Documented for what each court wrote; the inference from a founder to the organization today is Disputed.

**The Solis order and the trial exhibits (Document 1356 and about 27 trial documents, read October 22).** The July 1, 2009 memorandum opinion (20 pages, read in full) treated CAIR's brief as a motion to strike and seal, applied the Fifth Circuit's Briggs test, found that publicly naming 246 co-conspirators harmed CAIR with no legitimate government interest, and ordered the list sealed while declining to remove the names. It found that the government's four pieces of evidence "create at least a prima facie case as to CAIR's involvement in a conspiracy to support Hamas," noted that the government cited no occasion on which it used a CAIR statement under the co-conspirator hearsay rule, and wrote that the government "produced ample evidence to establish the associations of CAIR, ISNA and NAIT with HLF, the Islamic Association for Palestine, and with Hamas," while recognizing that the evidence largely predates the foundation's designation. The exhibits it cites: Government Exhibit 3-78, a July 30, 1994 Palestine Committee meeting agenda, read directly in the repo copy (handwritten Arabic with the government's translation), lists IAP, HLF and UASR on one line and, on a second line, "Coordination CAIR"; the court and the government call CAIR a working organization, but the line can also be read as coordination with CAIR, and an independent Arabic translation is still needed. Exhibit 3-85, the 1991 Explanatory Memorandum, lists the Society, the Trust, the Occupied Land Fund and UASR among Brotherhood organizations and friends; CAIR is not on it. Omar Ahmad: the Philadelphia transcript (October 1993) names him as a speaker, a September 13, 1993 wiretap has him, Ashqar and Baker planning the meeting, agent Burns identified him as the speaker who said the group sends "two messages, one to the Americans and one to the Muslims," and he is on the 35-name Palestine section phone list and the government's co-conspirator list. Nihad Awad: he is on that phone list (Minneapolis), and the planning call and the Philadelphia transcript mention a "Nihad" with no last name; nothing in these files identifies that person as Awad, and Awad is not on the government's co-conspirator list. Burns testified (September 29 and October 7, 2008) that CAIR did not exist before the Philadelphia meeting and was created after it "as a result of this," but the transcript's call for organizations without a conspicuous Islamic hue never names CAIR, so that link is her inference; the trial judge ruled that CAIR "is still not in this case" and called it a collateral issue. The Fifth Circuit later said, about the Trust, that the "ample evidence" statement went outside the bounds of the question and that the listing was an untested allegation; CAIR did not appeal. Ratings: Documented for what the order and exhibits say; Documented that Ahmad took part in Philadelphia and is on both lists; Reported that CAIR was created as a result of Philadelphia and that Exhibit 3-78 shows CAIR as a committee organ; Not established that Awad attended; Disputed that CAIR itself is a Hamas or Brotherhood affiliate. Most trial documents came from a partisan host and should be checked against the docket.

**Medicare for All and insurance jobs.** In an Instagram video (undated, transcribed in the project repo), El-Sayed says he does not think anyone needs to be banned from private insurance, and in the same answer says Medicare for All "does away with the private insurance market" and leaves one payer, the federal government. The two statements pull against each other; a single-payer system with no private market is what drives the job estimates below. Rating: Documented as his words. The best-known estimate comes from economist Robert Pollin of the Political Economy Research Institute at UMass Amherst: about 2 million mostly administrative jobs eliminated, roughly half on the insurance side ([KFF Health News, May 2019](https://kffhealthnews.org/elections/analysis-a-health-care-overhaul-could-kill-2-million-jobs-and-thats-ok/)). A secondary summary of the same analysis gives about 1.8 million, with about 800,000 at insurers, over two to four years ([People's World](https://www.peoplesworld.org/article/looking-after-workers-who-will-lose-their-jobs-under-medicare-for-all/), an advocacy outlet). Offsetting gains are possible in nursing and care roles, which KFF notes pay less. Checked against the bill text: the current Sanders bill (S. 1506, introduced April 29, 2025) has a temporary worker assistance section (Title VI, Section 602) for people who run the insurance system and may lose jobs. It covers up to five years, sets aside at least 1 percent of the national health budget, and lists wage replacement, retirement benefits, job training and placement, preferential hiring and education benefits, with no dollar amounts or age rules (Congress.gov). The People's World description of full salary to 65 for workers 60 and older, and a year's salary plus $10,000, does not appear in that text; it may describe an earlier version and should not be relied on. The industry-funded Partnership for America's Health Care Future uses the same figure to argue for layoffs, treating jobs "no longer needed" as net loss ([Partnership](https://americashealthcarefuture.org/icymi-medicare-for-all-means-layoffs/)). A separate Stanford estimate puts 860,000 to 1.5 million jobs at risk from hospital revenue losses, a different mechanism that probably overlaps. Rating: Reported, a projection from one research group and not a measured result. No Michigan-specific projection was found. For scale only: insurance carriers and related activities, which include non-health insurers, employed about 87,000 people in Michigan in December 2025, up from about 69,600 in January 2015 (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis, federal labor data), and Blue Cross Blue Shield of Michigan reports 12,196 employees worldwide, with 220 laid off in June 2025 after a $1 billion loss in 2024 (BCBSM; Becker's Payer). Congress would have to pass any such bill.
