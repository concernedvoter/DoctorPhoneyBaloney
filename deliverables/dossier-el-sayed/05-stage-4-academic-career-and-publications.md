## Stage 4: Academic career and publications

His scholarship is mainstream social epidemiology: obesity inequality, systems modeling and Arab-American health. None of the papers reviewed here addresses Islamic law or political Islam.

**Research profile (added October 24, 2026).** A separate profile checks his roughly 70 PubMed-indexed papers for racial, national-origin and political framing, and tests the October 2026 accusations against the papers' own text: deliverables/research-profile-el-sayed.md.

**What he favors (added October 24, 2026).** His own words and work show consistent leanings, stated openly. The record also holds findings or statements that cut against several of them.

- **Government-guaranteed health care.** Medicare for All is his central cause. He co-wrote *Medicare for All: A Citizen's Guide* (Oxford University Press, 2021), and his platform says Medicare should be "automatic, for everyone, and accepted everywhere" (Reported). None of his research compared payment systems. Documented.
- **Workers over corporate and big-donor money.** He backs union rights, abolishing medical debt and tougher antitrust enforcement. In 2018 he said Michiganders "are sick of corporate politicians who are bought and sold," and in 2026 he made AIPAC and corporate PACs a constant target. Money in politics is his most consistent target, more than any group of people. Documented (as reported by Bridge Michigan).
- **Public institutions and prevention.** Health departments, federal research funding and prevention; he wrote in 2016 that "government is the most efficient provider of public goods." Documented. Bridge Michigan found he overstated the Wayne County medical-debt program: he claimed $700 million erased for 300,000 residents, while the county had paid off $57.4 million for 83,291 residents so far. Reported.
- **Immigrants, Arab Americans and Muslims.** 18 of his 70 research papers concern Arab Americans, and he presents himself as a child of immigrants. He has said, "I'm not running to be a Muslim senator. I'm running for US Senate and happen to be Muslim" (Reported). His research also faulted Arab tradition for shorter lives and found no immigrant health advantage among Arab Americans in California. Documented.
- **Reproductive rights.** Endorsed by Planned Parenthood Action Fund; pledges to protect abortion, contraception and IVF and to repeal the Hyde Amendment. Documented.
- **Gaza and Israel policy.** He calls the Gaza war a genocide, joined the 2024 "uncommitted" vote and campaigns against AIPAC. See Adjacent controversies and Rhetoric. Documented.
- **Beyond his coalition.** To hunters at a Midland town hall on October 17, 2026: "If you're using your shotgun for hunting, nobody wants to take that away from you." In the same remarks he joked about "equal rights to a musket." Reported.

The full research analysis, including what his papers favor and the findings against each, is in the separate research profile linked above.

**Full text checked: 2009 Arab-American health review.** The paper "The health of Arab-Americans living in the United States: a systematic review of the literature" (Abdulrahman M El-Sayed and Sandro Galea, BMC Public Health 2009;9:272) summarizes published research on disease burden and its determinants among Arab-Americans and concludes that more research is warranted given the group's marginalization in the post-9/11 climate. A read of the full text found no mention of Hamas or the Brotherhood. The words Egypt and Palestine appear once each in describing immigration waves, and "terrorist" appears in discussing studies of birth outcomes after the September 11 attacks (PubMed Central). It is a conventional epidemiology review. Rating: Documented.

**Two more 2011 Michigan studies, with Galea.** "Ethnic inequalities in mortality: the case of Arab-Americans" (El-Sayed, Tracy, Scarborough and Galea, PLoS One 2011;6(12):e29185) compares life expectancy and age-adjusted mortality of Arab-Americans with non-Arab, non-Hispanic whites in Michigan and, as already listed, finds Arab-American life expectancy lower. "Suicide among Arab-Americans" (same authors, PLoS One 2011;6(2):e14704) examines Michigan suicide deaths from 1990 to 2007 against 2000 Census denominators. It found suicide rates 51 percent lower among Arab-American men and 33 percent lower among women than among non-ethnic whites, and suggests Arab ethnicity and ethnic density may be protective (PubMed). Both are routine public health studies of Michigan death records; neither concerns political groups, Hamas or the Brotherhood. Rating: Documented.

I read it through a summarizing tool, so section details should be checked against the PDF before quoting. It gives his birth as October 31, 1984, in Michigan, and lists citizenship as USA and Egypt. Education: BS in biology and political science, University of Michigan, 2003 to 2007, highest distinction; DPhil in public health, Oxford (Oriel College), October 2009 to October 2011, on inequalities in obesity in England; MD, Columbia College of Physicians and Surgeons, listed as in progress for August 2007 to May 2014, with a note of transfer from Michigan (consistent with his Michigan start and later move to the Columbia dual-degree program, which the CV dates 2008 to 2014). Appointments: Project Coordinator in epidemiology at Columbia, October 2011 to May 2014; Assistant Professor, same department, from May 2014; Research Associate, Oxford, from June 2012; Research Assistant at Michigan, 2007 to 2009. Non-academic: associate editor and fellowship director at The 2x2 Project (from December 2011), fellow at Demos (from September 2011), and MCAT director at Cambridge Coaching (from April 2012). Honors: Rhodes Scholarship (2009), Marshall Scholarship (2009, declined), Paul and Daisy Soros Fellowship (2012). Output: 35 published peer-reviewed articles (2007 to 2013), two book chapters, and 57 public commentary pieces from 2010 to 2013, mostly in the Huffington Post, the Guardian and The 2x2 Project, including Middle East and Egypt topics. The CV does not mention a medical license or a residency, and it lists no languages or references. Rating: Documented as the content of the document. Two cautions: a CV "updated October 2013" that lists a May 2014 professorship and a 2014 chaired session suggests it was edited or projected forward after that date, and the document's origin and posting date are not established beyond the Free Beacon's copy. The 57 commentary pieces are a lead: they are a dated, public record of his 2010 to 2013 writing on Egypt, listed in the open-leads file for retrieval.

**Childhood residence: Missouri and Florida (added October 23).** His campaign presents him as a lifelong Michigander, and a campaign video says he grew up "as Michigan as you get." A June 23, 2026 piece by Anna Hoffman on the site Michigan Enjoyer places much of his early childhood outside Michigan: a Missouri Social Security number issued in 1987, a December 1991 Boone County, Missouri court name change, and family records placing him in Florida with his father until at least 1995, citing a 2017 Guardian profile ([Michigan Enjoyer](https://enjoyer.com/abdul-el-sayed-parents-childhood/)). The piece gives his birth as October 31, 1984, in Detroit, which matches the CV. Newly circulated photographs are said to show him at Russell Boulevard Elementary in Columbia, Missouri, around 1991; Columbia is in Boone County, which fits that timeline, but the photographs have not been reviewed here. Relevance: none to eligibility, since the Constitution sets no birthplace or childhood residence requirement for senators; the point is whether the "lifelong Michigander" framing is complete. Rating: Reported (one critical blog citing records not reviewed here; the Guardian profile not yet read). Open: read the Guardian profile and any statement he has made about his early childhood. Parents' records are summarized, as context only, in the Stage 1 section above.

**"As Michigan as you get": what the record shows (added October 24, 2026).** The phrase is a campaign self-description, not a statement of birth fact. The question is whether it is complete for his childhood. The test below separates what is documented from what is not.

- **Documented Michigan ties.** University of Michigan student and degrees, 2003 to 2009 (Stage 2); the university's 2008 release calling him a first-generation Egyptian American from Bloomfield Hills; a 2007 student commencement address reported by the Michigan Daily; Detroit health director, 2015 to 2017; Wayne County health director, 2023 to 2025.
- **Documented Missouri tie in childhood.** A Boone County, Missouri court changed his name on December 9, 1991, when he was a minor, on a petition brought by a next friend, Mohamed El-Sayed (case 1391CC044966; published notice December 1991 to January 1992). Venue suggests a Boone County residence, but the docket does not state where he lived.
- **Reported, not verified.** The New York Times (Oct 21, 2026) says he was raised in Missouri, Florida and Michigan by his father and a stepmother. Michigan Enjoyer places him in Florida until at least 1995 and in a Columbia, Missouri school around 1991; the photographs have not been reviewed here.
- **Not established.** Michigan Enjoyer also cites a Missouri Social Security number issued in 1987. No source for that was reviewed, a Social Security number is a private identifier, and it is not used as evidence here. An SSN issue state would show where an application was made, not where he was born.
- **Birthplace.** Not independently verified: Rochester Hills (Bridge Michigan), Bloomfield Hills (University of Michigan), "Metro Detroit" (Soros page). The birth record is sealed. No document found states a foreign birth.
- **Fair wording.** Supported: he has documented adult ties to Michigan, and records and reporting place part of his childhood in Missouri and Florida. Not supported: that he was born outside Michigan, that his childhood claims are false, or that the phrase was meant to mislead. The campaign has not been asked, in the material reviewed here, to say where he lived as a child.
- **Relevance.** None to eligibility. The Constitution sets no birthplace or childhood-residence requirement for senators. The point is the completeness of a campaign biography.
- **To resolve.** Ask the campaign in writing where he lived, by age, from birth to 1995, and whether the phrase refers to birth, family roots or later residence. Rating: adult Michigan ties and the 1991 Missouri court case Documented; childhood residence in Missouri and Florida Reported; the SSN claim not used.
