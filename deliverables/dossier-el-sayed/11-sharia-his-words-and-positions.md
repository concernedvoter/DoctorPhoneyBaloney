## Sharia: his words and positions

He follows Sharia in personal matters such as finance and opposes legal bans on Sharia. No statement or proposal found in this review calls for religious law in U.S. law, and his campaign says he opposes that.

**Positions that cut against a Sharia agenda**

- Supports codifying Roe v. Wade and the Women's Health Protection Act ([guides.vote](https://guides.vote/?p=5672)).
- Supports trans rights and pledges to be "a lion" for LGBTQ rights ([Pride Source](https://pridesource.com/article/abdul-el-sayed-2026-primary-election-interview)); endorsed by the Human Rights Campaign PAC ([HRC](https://www.hrc.org/press-releases/human-rights-campaign-pac-endorses-pro-equality-champion-abdul-el-sayed-for-u-s-senate)).
- Some Dearborn Muslim voters told Fox they oppose these positions but back him anyway ([Fox via WFMD](https://www.wfmd.com/?p=80550)).

**Constitutional context**

- Oklahoma's 2010 ban never took effect. Federal courts blocked it as likely violating the Establishment Clause by singling out Islam; the Tenth Circuit affirmed in 2012 and the ban was struck down in 2013 ([Islamic Law Blog](https://islamiclaw.blog/2018/04/30/islamic-law-in-u-s-courts-awad-v-ziriax-10th-cir-2012-anti-shari%ca%bfa-amendment-to-state-constitution/); [PolitiFact](https://politifact.com/factchecks/2026/aug/10/tweets/abdul-el-sayed-sharia-muslim-cair-michigan-senate/)).
- The challenger was CAIR-Oklahoma's director, Muneer Awad ([MEAWW](https://news.meaww.com/fact-check-did-el-sayed-call-for-the-implementation-of-sharia-law-in-the-us)). His opposition to the ban matches the courts; his framing of its supporters is the contestable part.
- Private religious practice, including religious finance, is constitutionally protected. Article VI bars any religious test for federal office, while voters remain free to weigh a candidate's views.
- **Background: what the Egyptian Muslim Brotherhood's own platforms state.** Katerina Dalacoura (LSE), "Islamism, secularization, secularity: the Muslim Brotherhood in Egypt as a phenomenon of a secular age," Economy and Society (2018), a peer-reviewed article analyzing the Brotherhood's 2004, 2005, 2007 and 2010 Egyptian platform documents. It is not about El-Sayed or CAIR and is included only as background on an organization referenced elsewhere in this dossier. Per this source: the Brotherhood's own 2007 platform states Islam "rejects a theocratic state in favor of a civil one" while also calling sharia "the main source of legislation," pursued through legal and constitutional channels rather than by force; its stated method across these documents is electoral and gradualist ("building the Muslim individual, Muslim family, Muslim government"), not violent conquest. This confirms the Brotherhood's platforms genuinely call for sharia-based legislation, which is a documented goal and not a fabrication; it also confirms the movement's own stated method is legal and electoral, which cuts against characterizations of it as pursuing violent takeover. Note: this is the Egyptian Muslim Brotherhood specifically. CAIR is a separate U.S. nonprofit; Executive Order 14362 designates only the Egyptian, Jordanian and Lebanese Brotherhood chapters, not CAIR or any U.S. organization. Nothing in this source names El-Sayed, CAIR, or any U.S. political figure.

**Assessment.** Documented: personal religious observance, opposition to Sharia bans, and rhetoric tying those bans to white supremacy. Not supported: any advocacy to impose Sharia through U.S. law.
