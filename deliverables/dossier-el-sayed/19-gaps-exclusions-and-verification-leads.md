## Gaps, exclusions and verification leads

These questions could change the story. A records check on October 19, 2026 resolved some and narrowed others; the status opens each item.

**Not found in this review**

- Any statement, bill or platform plank by him calling for Sharia in U.S. law.
- Membership in, funding of, or a pledge to the Muslim Brotherhood.
- Ties to the International Institute of Islamic Thought, the Muslim Public Affairs Council, or Muslim medical associations.
- Any public statement on the 2017 Detroit-area female genital mutilation case.

**Excluded on purpose**

- Unrelated legal matters of private relatives and of a former campaign aide. They describe other people's conduct, not his.
- Personal networks of donors and relatives beyond the second step.

**Open questions and where to check**

1. **2012 Morsi letter: narrowed.** The Al-Ahram original lists a Dr. Abdel Rahman El-Sayed of New York as signer No. 33 of 46. The New York Times (Oct 21, 2026) reports that he signed a 2012 open letter while in medical school and that his aides declined to answer questions about it, so the signer identity now rests on the Times's reporting and the name matches, not only the Free Beacon's unnamed sources; the campaign has not confirmed or explained it. Ask the campaign why he signed.
2. **Mother's IARA job: narrowed.** The New York Times (Oct 21, 2026) confirms 1999 to 2004 from her own interview account and describes her as a low-ranking employee who was never accused of wrongdoing; no role or title is public. The government's June 23, 2010 witness list in the case (Document 518) names her a probable witness, described as a former employee who would testify about her work there; it is a list of people who may be called, and whether she testified is not established here. Still open: what the 13 court exhibits that concern her show, which the Times says are letters documenting transactions from 1999 to 2001 whose contents are not in the public record it reviewed.
3. Whether he holds Egyptian nationality by descent regardless of documents. Ask an Egyptian nationality-law specialist, and ask the campaign whether he has ever held an Egyptian ID or passport.
4. **CAIR-Oklahoma fee: narrowed.** His Senate reports filed in June 2025 and July 2026 both list CAIR Oklahoma as a source of more than $5,000 for a speaking engagement, with no amount or date ([2025 report](https://michiganadvance.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/eFD-Annual-Report-for-2025-El-Sayed-Abdul.pdf); [2026 report](https://freebeacon.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/eFD_-Annual-Report-for-2026-El-Sayed-Abdul.pdf)). That part of a candidate report generally reaches back about two years, which may place the payment after the October 2022 keynote. Confirm with the Senate Ethics Committee's instructions or the campaign.
5. **Tracy, Calif., donor: still open.** FEC data still list the July 20, 2026 $100,000 gift to the Unity and Justice Fund that way, while every other record for the name, including $300,000 to Fighting for Michigan, lists Shelby Township. Ask the fund's treasurer whether the address is a filing error.
6. **Money after August 31: partly answered.** Both committees' September reports are due October 20. Their 24-hour notices since then are in the money map. CAIR Action's total to the fund is $185,000 in FEC data, which settles the earlier discrepancy.
7. **2018 forum video: found.** Bridge Michigan posted the full May 10, 2018 forum on [Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/BridgeMichigan/videos/1659043040818235/). Watch the exchange in full before quoting it. No record of a separate Mackinac exchange was found; that label may be mistaken.

- **Fig and Olive Action: who gave the $370,000, and why its funders may stay hidden.** American Priorities' report lists the July 13, 2026 gift from "Fig and Olive Action" of Wilmington as an organization with no committee ID (FEC, line 11AI, checked October 20), so it came from the nonprofit, Fig and Olive Action Inc, not from its PAC. InfluenceWatch attributes the gift to the PAC; the filing does not support that, and the PAC had raised only $25,048. InfluenceWatch lists the PAC as a nonqualified membership-organization committee, which may solicit only its members, their executives and their families. Since a 2020 IRS rule, 501(c)(4) groups do not report donor names to the IRS, and a super PAC need only name the nonprofit that gave it money. The donors would become reportable only if they gave the nonprofit money for the purpose of funding the super PAC, which would make them the true contributors; federal law bars giving in another person's name (52 U.S.C. 30122). Nothing on the record shows that. The questions to ask are when Fig and Olive Action Inc received its money relative to July 13, and whether it does anything besides political giving. Rating: source of the $370,000 Documented (FEC); its funders unknown.
