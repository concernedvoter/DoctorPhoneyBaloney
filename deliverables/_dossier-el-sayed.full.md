# Abdul El-Sayed Dossier: Record, Money and Ties

Prepared 2026-10-18

## Bottom line

The record shows multiple, mostly public ties between El-Sayed and two Muslim American institutions, CAIR and ISNA. They run through speeches, donors, super PAC money and his father-in-law, a former CAIR-Michigan president.

It does not show him advocating that Sharia replace or override U.S. law. [PolitiFact](https://politifact.com/factchecks/2026/aug/10/tweets/abdul-el-sayed-sharia-muslim-cair-michigan-senate/) rated claims that he called for Sharia in America Pants on Fire, and [Bridge Michigan](https://www.bridgemi.com/michigan-government/fact-check-no-evidence-abdul-el-sayed-pledged-support-to-muslim-brotherhood/) and [Fox News Digital](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/unearthed-clip-exposes-clash-between-el-sayed-ex-gop-rival-muslims-hate-you) found no evidence of a personal Muslim Brotherhood tie. His platform, including abortion rights and LGBTQ rights, runs against a Sharia-governance agenda.

The contested ground is rhetoric, old writing on Egypt, and family. He tied a 2010 Oklahoma Sharia ban to white supremacy and wrote supportively of Egypt's 2011 revolution. His half-sister has posted that their mother was a Brotherhood activist in the early 1970s; he denies any family ties. His sharpest documented words are a 2018 retort to a rival who accused him of "civilization jihad," a March 2026 response to a synagogue attack that he later apologized for, and personal jabs at opponents; fact-checkers found no pattern of disrespecting women. See Rhetoric on race, gender and Israel.

**Method.** Eight stages of his life, then the money, Sharia, the Brotherhood and each organization's legal status. Links are followed while they stay documented and involve public figures or organizations. Past the third step they stop being evidence about him, so the trace stops there.

**Evidence ratings used below**

- Documented: his own words on record, filings or primary documents.
- Reported: one outlet's finding not independently confirmed here; treat as a lead.
- Disputed: contested by fact-checkers or by other evidence.
- Not supported: found false, or no evidence located in this review.

## Stage 1: Family and upbringing

He is the son of Egyptian immigrants. The University of Michigan described him in 2008 as a first-generation Egyptian American from Bloomfield Hills ([University of Michigan](https://news.umich.edu/u-m-dual-md-phd-student-named-rhodes-scholar)). Bridge Michigan gives his full name as Abdulrahman Mohamed El-Sayed, born October 31, 1984 in Rochester Hills (Bridge Michigan; Reported). The Michigan Daily named him Abdulrahman Mohamed El Sayed at his 2007 commencement, which matches the name. A birth record could not be checked, since Michigan birth records are not open to the public, and Bloomfield Hills is the hometown the university cites.

- **Father.** The Free Beacon describes him as an engineering professor at Eastern Michigan University ([Free Beacon](https://freebeacon.com/?p=2166280)). In his memoir, El-Sayed wrote that his father left Egypt in 1978 partly over political agitation against its military government ([Free Beacon](https://freebeacon.com/democrats/abdul-el-sayeds-family-has-long-been-in-egyptian-muslim-brotherhood-senate-candidates-sister-said-in-unearthed-social-media-posts/)).

The New York Times adds that he was raised in Missouri, Florida and Michigan by his father and a stepmother, and that she is now a psychiatric nurse practitioner in Missouri (New York Times, Oct 21, 2026). Her later daughter, his half-sister Eman Abdelhadi, is a University of Chicago professor whose posts are covered in the Brotherhood section.
- **Egypt.** He has spoken of childhood visits to his father's family in Alexandria ([Free Beacon](https://freebeacon.com/?p=2166901)).
- **Hometown****.** Bloomfield Hills, per the University of Michigan's 2008 release ([University of Michigan](https://news.umich.edu/u-m-dual-md-phd-student-named-rhodes-scholar)).

Rating: Documented, except the father's current academic post (Reported).

On that rule he was an Egyptian national from birth if his father was Egyptian when he was born. Under either route he was eligible in this period; whether he registered or was issued Egyptian documents is not known.

## Stage 4: Academic career and publications

His scholarship is mainstream social epidemiology: obesity inequality, systems modeling and Arab-American health. None of the papers reviewed here addresses Islamic law or political Islam.

**Full text checked: 2009 Arab-American health review.** The paper "The health of Arab-Americans living in the United States: a systematic review of the literature" (Abdulrahman M El-Sayed and Sandro Galea, BMC Public Health 2009;9:272) summarizes published research on disease burden and its determinants among Arab-Americans and concludes that more research is warranted given the group's marginalization in the post-9/11 climate. A read of the full text found no mention of Hamas or the Brotherhood. The words Egypt and Palestine appear once each in describing immigration waves, and "terrorist" appears in discussing studies of birth outcomes after the September 11 attacks (PubMed Central). It is a conventional epidemiology review. Rating: Documented.

**Two more 2011 Michigan studies, with Galea.** "Ethnic inequalities in mortality: the case of Arab-Americans" (El-Sayed, Tracy, Scarborough and Galea, PLoS One 2011;6(12):e29185) compares life expectancy and age-adjusted mortality of Arab-Americans with non-Arab, non-Hispanic whites in Michigan and, as already listed, finds Arab-American life expectancy lower. "Suicide among Arab-Americans" (same authors, PLoS One 2011;6(2):e14704) examines Michigan suicide deaths from 1990 to 2007 against 2000 Census denominators. It found suicide rates 51 percent lower among Arab-American men and 33 percent lower among women than among non-ethnic whites, and suggests Arab ethnicity and ethnic density may be protective (PubMed). Both are routine public health studies of Michigan death records; neither concerns political groups, Hamas or the Brotherhood. Rating: Documented.

**Curriculum vitae, dated October 2013 (****Free Beacon copy, PDF****).** A 12-page academic CV under the name Abdulrahman M. El-Sayed, DPhil. I read it through a summarizing tool, so section details should be checked against the PDF before quoting. It gives his birth as October 31, 1984, in Michigan, and lists citizenship as USA and Egypt. Education: BS in biology and political science, University of Michigan, 2003 to 2007, highest distinction; DPhil in public health, Oxford (Oriel College), October 2009 to October 2011, on inequalities in obesity in England; MD, Columbia College of Physicians and Surgeons, listed as in progress for August 2007 to May 2014, with a note of transfer from Michigan (consistent with his Michigan start and later move to the Columbia dual-degree program, which the CV dates 2008 to 2014). Appointments: Project Coordinator in epidemiology at Columbia, October 2011 to May 2014; Assistant Professor, same department, from May 2014; Research Associate, Oxford, from June 2012; Research Assistant at Michigan, 2007 to 2009. Non-academic: associate editor and fellowship director at The 2x2 Project (from December 2011), fellow at Demos (from September 2011), and MCAT director at Cambridge Coaching (from April 2012). Honors: Rhodes Scholarship (2009), Marshall Scholarship (2009, declined), Paul and Daisy Soros Fellowship (2012). Output: 35 published peer-reviewed articles (2007 to 2013), two book chapters, and 57 public commentary pieces from 2010 to 2013, mostly in the Huffington Post, the Guardian and The 2x2 Project, including Middle East and Egypt topics. The CV does not mention a medical license or a residency, and it lists no languages or references. Rating: Documented as the content of the document. Two cautions: a CV "updated October 2013" that lists a May 2014 professorship and a 2014 chaired session suggests it was edited or projected forward after that date, and the document's origin and posting date are not established beyond the Free Beacon's copy. The 57 commentary pieces are a lead: they are a dated, public record of his 2010 to 2013 writing on Egypt, listed in the open-leads file for retrieval.

**Childhood residence: Missouri and Florida (added October 23).** His campaign presents him as a lifelong Michigander, and a campaign video says he grew up "as Michigan as you get." A June 23, 2026 piece by Anna Hoffman on the site Michigan Enjoyer places much of his early childhood outside Michigan: a Missouri Social Security number issued in 1987, a December 1991 Boone County, Missouri court name change, and family records placing him in Florida with his father until at least 1995, citing a 2017 Guardian profile ([Michigan Enjoyer](https://enjoyer.com/abdul-el-sayed-parents-childhood/)). Relevance: none to eligibility, since the Constitution sets no birthplace or childhood residence requirement for senators; the point is whether the "lifelong Michigander" framing is complete. Rating: Reported (one critical blog citing records not reviewed here; the Guardian profile not yet read). Open: read the Guardian profile and any statement he has made about his early childhood. Parents' personal records (divorce, naturalization) are not used here because they concern private people.

**Health rankings during his tenure.** He led the Detroit city department, not the Wayne County Health Department, which Dr. Mouhanad Hammami headed at the time (The Nation's Health, July 2016). The County Health Rankings compare Wayne County only with other Michigan counties, and Detroit is not ranked separately. Wayne County was 83 of 83 for health outcomes and for health factors in 2016 (Detroit News, Crain's Detroit; Reported) and again in 2017 (County Health Rankings 2017 Michigan report; Documented). It was also last in 2013 and 81 of 82 on outcomes in 2011, and later releases reportedly show it last in 2020 and 2022. No county-level national ranking exists; Michigan as a state was 35th, 34th and 35th of 50 in America's Health Rankings for 2015 to 2017. The 2015 and 2018 county ranks are not yet verified.

**Outcomes versus attribution.** Detroit infant deaths per 1,000 births were 13.2 (2014), 14.4 (2015), 13.3 (2016), 14.2 (2017), 16.7 (2018) and 11.0 (2019) (Michigan Department of Health and Human Services); year-to-year changes sit within the margin of error of about 2.3, and the 2016 national rate was 5.87 (CDC). **Court documents on the co-conspirator list and a CAIR co-founder (read in full October 22).** Three filings now sit in the repo's public-records folder. (1) United States v. Holy Land Foundation (North American Islamic Trust, movant), No. 09-10875, Fifth Circuit, October 20, 2010: the government's 2007 trial brief attached a list of 246 unindicted co-conspirators and joint venturers, filed publicly by what the government called an oversight, with the Trust listed under a heading for people or groups who "are and/or were members of the U.S. Muslim Brotherhood"; the brief called Hamas the Palestinian branch of the Brotherhood. The Fifth Circuit affirmed that the listing was a procedural error because the list should have been sealed, ordered the district court's opinion unsealed, and let stand the refusal to remove the Trust's name. It also held that the Trust's inclusion was "simply an untested allegation of the Government," that no co-conspirator ruling about the Trust was ever made, and that the district judge's statement of "ample evidence" linking the Trust to the Holy Land Foundation, the Islamic Association for Palestine and Hamas went outside the bounds of what was needed and was not grounded in a legal standard with substance and boundaries. It said the findings do not amount to a ruling that the Trust joined a criminal conspiracy, and the government itself disclaimed calling the Trust a criminal co-conspirator. A footnote says CAIR moved to file a brief asking that all names be stricken, that the motion was addressed in the same sealed order, and that CAIR was not a party to the appeal. (2) United States v. El-Mezain, No. 09-10560, Fifth Circuit, December 7, 2011 (170 pages, read in full): it does not name CAIR, but it says Omar Ahmad was a member of the Palestine Committee and associated with the Islamic Association for Palestine, and that 1999 wiretaps caught him directing Holy Land Foundation business, which the court took to suggest the committee controlled the foundation even after Hamas was designated in 1995. (3) Judicial Watch v. Department of Justice, 898 F. Supp. 2d 93 (D.D.C. Oct. 12, 2012), a records case that describes Omar Ahmad as a co-founder of CAIR; it notes the government has never formally pursued his prosecution, and the court accepted the Justice Department's refusal to confirm or deny any records of a decision not to prosecute him. Taken together: Documented that a CAIR co-founder is described by the Fifth Circuit as a Palestine Committee member, which the same opinion says the Brotherhood directed to support Hamas; Documented that CAIR appears in the co-conspirator controversy; Not supported that any court convicted CAIR or found it to be a criminal co-conspirator (Judge Solis found only a prima facie case of its involvement, described in the next paragraph); the ruling's "ample evidence" language, which JTA reports covered CAIR, the Islamic Society of North America and the Trust together, was criticized on appeal as going beyond the legal question, and the order itself (Document 1356, July 1, 2009) has now been read and is summarized in the next paragraph; JTA adds that the evidence the judge cited largely predates the foundation's 2001 designation, that some of it is shared officers and office space in the late 1980s, and that a 1991 Brotherhood strategy paper lists the Society and the Trust, but not CAIR, among its "friends". Ahmad was never charged, and the Palestine Committee findings are about the 1980s and 1990s. Rating: Documented for what each court wrote; the inference from a founder to the organization today is Disputed.

**The Solis order and the trial exhibits (Document 1356 and about 27 trial documents, read October 22).** The July 1, 2009 memorandum opinion (20 pages, read in full) treated CAIR's brief as a motion to strike and seal, applied the Fifth Circuit's Briggs test, found that publicly naming 246 co-conspirators harmed CAIR with no legitimate government interest, and ordered the list sealed while declining to remove the names. It found that the government's four pieces of evidence "create at least a prima facie case as to CAIR's involvement in a conspiracy to support Hamas," noted that the government cited no occasion on which it used a CAIR statement under the co-conspirator hearsay rule, and wrote that the government "produced ample evidence to establish the associations of CAIR, ISNA and NAIT with HLF, the Islamic Association for Palestine, and with Hamas," while recognizing that the evidence largely predates the foundation's designation. The exhibits it cites: Government Exhibit 3-78, a July 30, 1994 Palestine Committee meeting agenda, read directly in the repo copy (handwritten Arabic with the government's translation), lists IAP, HLF and UASR on one line and, on a second line, "Coordination CAIR"; the court and the government call CAIR a working organization, but the line can also be read as coordination with CAIR, and an independent Arabic translation is still needed. Exhibit 3-85, the 1991 Explanatory Memorandum, lists the Society, the Trust, the Occupied Land Fund and UASR among Brotherhood organizations and friends; CAIR is not on it. Omar Ahmad: the Philadelphia transcript (October 1993) names him as a speaker, a September 13, 1993 wiretap has him, Ashqar and Baker planning the meeting, agent Burns identified him as the speaker who said the group sends "two messages, one to the Americans and one to the Muslims," and he is on the 35-name Palestine section phone list and the government's co-conspirator list. Nihad Awad: he is on that phone list (Minneapolis), and the planning call and the Philadelphia transcript mention a "Nihad" with no last name; nothing in these files identifies that person as Awad, and Awad is not on the government's co-conspirator list. Burns testified (September 29 and October 7, 2008) that CAIR did not exist before the Philadelphia meeting and was created after it "as a result of this," but the transcript's call for organizations without a conspicuous Islamic hue never names CAIR, so that link is her inference; the trial judge ruled that CAIR "is still not in this case" and called it a collateral issue. The Fifth Circuit later said, about the Trust, that the "ample evidence" statement went outside the bounds of the question and that the listing was an untested allegation; CAIR did not appeal. Ratings: Documented for what the order and exhibits say; Documented that Ahmad took part in Philadelphia and is on both lists; Reported that CAIR was created as a result of Philadelphia and that Exhibit 3-78 shows CAIR as a committee organ; Not established that Awad attended; Disputed that CAIR itself is a Hamas or Brotherhood affiliate. ## Medical license and the health officer posts

**Bottom line.** Michigan law let him serve as a health officer without a medical license, as an administrative health officer working with a licensed medical director. On the public record he appears to have had less public health administrative experience than the written rule requires at both appointments, unless the state credited other experience or approved a provisional or acting appointment; those records were not found. No record reviewed shows he practiced medicine, and no complaint or charge over his use of titles has been reported. This is not legal advice.

**His credentials.** MD, Columbia, class of 2014; DPhil, Oxford. He withdrew his residency application and has never held a medical license in Michigan or New York ([Columbia](https://www.vagelos.columbia.edu/node/23958); [PolitiFact](https://politifact.com/article/2026/oct/14/abdul-el-sayed-doctor-physician-michigan-senate/)).

**The posts.** Detroit, August 2015 to February 2017: the state listed him as the Detroit Health Department's executive director and health officer ([State of Michigan](https://www.michigan.gov/formergovernors/recent/snyder/press-releases/2016/06/10/gov--rick-snyder-appoints-members-to-new-child-lead-poisoning-elimination-board)). Wayne County, 2023 to April 2025: director of Health, Human and Veterans Services and the county's health officer; Dr. Avani Sheth, the department's chief medical officer, became interim health officer when he left ([CBS Detroit](https://www.cbsnews.com/detroit/news/wayne-county-health-director-abdul-el-sayed-resigns/)).

**What the law requires**

- Each local health department must have a full-time health officer with qualifications set by the state health department ([MCL 333.2428](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=MCL-333-2428)).
- A health officer is either a medical health officer, who must be a licensed physician, or an administrative health officer ([R 325.13001 to R 325.13003](https://gsaindexed.apps.lara.state.mi.us/ORR/103_61_AdminCode.pdf)).
- An administrative health officer needs an MPH or MSPH and 3 years of full-time public health administrative experience, a related graduate degree and 5 years, or a bachelor's degree and 8 years, 5 of them administering public health programs (R 325.13003).
- When the health officer is not a physician, the department must also employ a medical director who is a licensed physician (R 325.13001 and R 325.13004).
- Provisional appointments of up to 3 years need state approval and a public health physician advisor; acting appointments run 6 months, renewable, with state approval (R 325.13007 and R 325.13008).

**How he fits.** Without a license he could not be a medical health officer. His Oxford doctorate could count as a related graduate degree, which carries the 5-year requirement. His public record shows no health department experience before Detroit and about 18 months of it before Wayne County, short even of the 3 years required with a public health master's degree. Unless the state credited other experience, such as his Columbia research program, or approved a provisional or acting appointment, he did not meet the written standard. The approval records are not online.

**Titles and the law**

- MCL 333.16263 reserves doctor of medicine and M.D. for licensees. Physician alone is not on the list, and Dr. is not restricted ([MCL 333.16263](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=MCL-333-16263)).
- MCL 333.16261 bars an unlicensed person from using any title in a way that would lead people to believe the person is licensed in Michigan ([MCL 333.16261](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Laws/MCL?objectName=MCL-333-16261)).
