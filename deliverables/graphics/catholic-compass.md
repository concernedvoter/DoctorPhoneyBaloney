# A Catholic compass

Two-axis placement of Mike Rogers and Abdul El-Sayed against Church teaching. Judgment from the rated record (see catholic-scorecard.md). The Church does not endorse candidates.

- Horizontal axis: life and family teaching (abortion, IVF, euthanasia, marriage and gender), opposed on the left to aligned on the right. The bishops call abortion the pre-eminent priority, so this axis carries more weight.
- Vertical axis: social teaching, distant at the bottom to aligned at the top.
- Quadrants: top-right is "closest to Church teaching on both"; top-left is farther on life and family, closer on social; bottom-right is closer on life and family, farther on social; bottom-left is far on both.
- Rogers (Republican): lower-right quadrant, closer on life and family, farther on social teaching.
- El-Sayed (Democrat): upper-left quadrant, farther on life and family, closer on social teaching.
- Neither is placed in the top-right quadrant.

**Rogers.** Closer: pro-life House record, marriage, religious liberty, legitimate self-defense. Farther: pledge to leave Michigan's abortion amendment alone, IVF mandate, early Iran war support, 2014 torture defense; the bishops also question current deportation practice.

**El-Sayed.** Closer: opposing the Iran war, migrants and a path to citizenship, health access, labor, creation. Farther: abortion rights, IVF, gender policy for minors, abolishing ICE, replacing the right to bear arms, his 2012 letter urging Morsi to purge the media and police, statements that conflict with the record.

Neither has a stated position found on euthanasia, the death penalty or school choice.

**How the bishops ask Catholics to weigh it.** Abortion and euthanasia are intrinsic evils and carry special weight, so the horizontal axis counts more. Immigration, health care, war, guns and creation are matters of prudential judgment: Catholics may disagree on means, not on principles. When every candidate falls short, Faithful Citizenship (paragraphs 34 to 37) says a voter may choose the one judged less likely to advance grave harms, and should weigh character and integrity too.

Sources: USCCB, Forming Consciences for Faithful Citizenship and Introductory Note (2023); Catechism of the Catholic Church; USCCB pastoral message on immigration (Nov. 2025); Pope Leo XIV statements (2026); candidate websites, debates and records as cited on the scorecard.
