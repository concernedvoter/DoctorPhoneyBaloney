El-Sayed Money Map

How money from Brotherhood-linked organizations and individuals reaches Abdul El-Sayed's Michigan Senate race, as of October 20, 2026. Each box is marked by what critics claim, not by what has been proven.

No money in this map comes from a person documented as a Muslim Brotherhood member, or from Egypt, Turkey, Qatar or any other country usually named in Brotherhood claims.

The orange boxes are CAIR and its channel, plus his father-in-law and a friend, Mohamed Soltan, whose father is described as a Brotherhood figure. CAIR has been designated by Texas, Florida and the UAE, and appears in Holy Land Foundation trial exhibits as an associated organization. It has no federal designation and was never charged.

That channel put about $475,000 into Fighting for Michigan PAC, 11 percent of the $4.4 million it had raised through August 31 (Jukaku $300,000 plus Unity and Justice Fund $175,000). The largest single source to that PAC is not in this channel: the Institute for Middle East Understanding gave $400,000, and no Brotherhood claim has been sourced for it.

American Priorities' three largest individual donors gave about $2.55 million, and all are reported to be former staff of AppLovin, a Nasdaq-listed Palo Alto company: Mohammad Waqas ($1.05 million), very likely Fig and Olive's treasurer Mohammad Waqas Javed; Omer Hasan ($1 million); and Tariq Afaq Ahmed ($500,000). Hasan and Ahmed also funded the super PAC that backed New York Mayor Zohran Mamdani. That is a shared donor network, not foreign money: no foreign-address donor appears in the super PACs' itemized records.

The $370,000 from Fig and Olive Action came from its nonprofit, which the FEC lists as an organization and which does not disclose its donors. He pledges to refuse super PAC money; American Priorities' $509,526 for him is independent spending he cannot legally direct, and as of July he had not publicly disavowed it.

Method and limits

Sources: Federal Election Commission itemized filings pulled through the FEC's public data service, the Senate financial disclosures, Free Beacon, Inside Political Money, CNN, JTA, Jewish Insider, InfluenceWatch (a project of the conservative Capital Research Center), Michigan Advance and the Florida Phoenix. A Wall Street Journal analysis of October 10 is cited only as relayed by Geller Report and Daily Wire, and is unverified. Brotherhood labels come from critics and governments and are rated as claims.

Rechecked against FEC records on October 20: the Fig and Olive and Justice Democrats gifts to American Priorities, the Institute for Middle East Understanding gifts, and Mansoor Ahmed's total.

Itemized data omits small donors and cannot show who is behind the Fig and Olive and American Priorities corporate gifts. Federal law bars contributions from foreign nationals; an itemized check of foreign-address donors found $300 net to the campaign and none to the three super PACs. FEC enforcement files stay confidential until a case closes.
